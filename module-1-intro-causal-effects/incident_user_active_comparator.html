Incident User & Active Comparator Designs

Module 1: Principles of Observational Study Design and Target Trial Emulation

Why Cross-Sectional Snapshots Fail

Comparing people currently on a treatment to people not on it introduces severe biases:

  • Prevalent User Bias: You only observe survivors who tolerated the drug and stayed on it. You miss early dropouts and adverse events.
  • Confounding by Indication: People on treatment differ systematically in severity and health-seeking habits compared to untreated people.
DESIGN 1

Incident User Design

Restrict study subjects strictly to new initiators of the treatment.

  • Washout Period: Require a baseline window (e.g., 6-12 months) with zero treatment use before start.
  • Synchronized Time Zero (T0): Align everyone's follow-up clock to the exact day of initiation.
  • Pre-Treatment Covariates: Measure confounders strictly before T0 so they are not affected by treatment.
  • What it fixes: Eliminates depletion of susceptibles, immortal time bias, and drop-out selection bias.
DESIGN 2

Active Comparator Design

Compare treatment of interest against an alternative active therapy for the same indication.

  • Same Indication: Both groups crossed the clinical threshold where a doctor decided treatment was needed.
  • Similar Behaviors: Both groups seek healthcare, fill prescriptions, and adhere to medical regimens.
  • Stronger Positivity: Eliminates extreme propensity scores because patients could reasonably receive either active drug.
  • Example: Yoga vs Zumba for hypertension, rather than Yoga vs sitting on the couch.

The Combined Standard: New-User Active-Comparator Design

Combining both methods creates a study design that closely emulates a pragmatic randomized trial:

Design Feature Naive Observational Study New-User Active-Comparator
Cohort Selection Any current user at survey time (prevalent users) Only new starters after a clear washout period
Control Group No treatment or placebo (unmeasured confounding) Active drug or exercise with matching indication
Time Zero (T0) Ambiguous, arbitrary calendar date Exact date of treatment initiation
Covariate Timing Often measured while already on treatment Measured strictly before initiation
